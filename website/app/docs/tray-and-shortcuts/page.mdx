# Tray & shortcuts

Most of what Helix does happens while its window is out of sight. The tray icon, notifications and global shortcuts are how you reach it then.

## The tray icon

While you are signed in, Helix puts an icon in the Windows notification area — on macOS, in the menu bar. It shows how many drives are up, and its menu reaches every drive without opening the window.

| Menu item | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Open Helix** | bring the window back |
| **Open folder** | a submenu of the drives that are mounted; pick one to open it |
| **Connect** / **Disconnect** *Z: — name* | one entry per drive, toggling it |
| **Connect all drives** / **Disconnect all drives** | every drive at once, without asking first |
| **Connect** *group* | one entry per [group](/docs/groups/) |
| **Exit Helix** | quit for real |

A tray action that fails says so in a notification and in the [diagnostic log](/docs/troubleshooting/), since there is no window to show a banner on.

## Notifications

Helix raises a system notification (on macOS, through Notification Center) when:

- a drive drops, and when the background reconnect brings it back,
- a NAS volume runs low on space (see the low-space warning in [Settings](/docs/settings/)),
- a [schedule](/docs/schedules/) runs,
- Helix hides itself in the tray for the first time in a session.

Results of things you do in the window — connecting a drive, saving a setting — appear as a banner at the top of the page instead, which dismisses itself.

## Closing the window

On Windows, two switches in Settings decide what the window's close button does.

| Setting | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Close to the system tray** | on | closing hides Helix to the tray, and it keeps reconnecting drops, warning about space and running schedules. Off makes the close button quit |
| **Notify when hidden to the tray** | on | the first time Helix hides itself each session, a notification says it is still running |

<Callout>
Helix only does its work while it is running. If you turn **Close to the system tray** off, nothing reconnects your drives once the window is closed. **Exit Helix** on the tray menu is the way to quit when it is on.
</Callout>

**Auto minimize app** (see [Settings](/docs/settings/)) hides the window to the tray after a countdown on the dashboard. Neither close switch is offered on macOS, where the window stays in the Dock.

## Global shortcuts

Switch on **Global shortcuts** in Settings to control your drives from any application:

| Shortcut | Action |
| --- | --- |
| <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>C</Kbd> | connect every drive |
| <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>D</Kbd> | disconnect every drive |

- Off by default.
- Three modifiers, because <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Alt</Kbd> is AltGr on many European keyboards, where AltGr+C and AltGr+D type characters.
- They do not ask for confirmation, and the result appears in a tray notification.
- They do nothing while you are signed out or while the [idle lock](/docs/settings/) is up.
- The keys are not swallowed, so another app bound to the same chord still sees them.
- On macOS, Helix needs Accessibility access for them to work.
